What is the Catholic Church's position on Medjugorje?

The Catholic Church permits pilgrimages to Medjugorje. In May 2019, Pope Francis authorized dioceses and parishes to organize them officially. Subsequently, in September 2024, a nihil obstat was granted for the spiritual experience associated with Medjugorje, recognizing its positive fruits and permitting public worship. This does not mean that the Church has declared the alleged apparitions authentic or supernatural, nor does it require the faithful to believe in them.

What documents do I need to travel to Medjugorje and the Holy Land if I have a green card or a passport from another country?

Requirements depend on your passport nationality, your residency and the countries you transit through. You must have a valid passport and, if you are a U.S. permanent resident, also carry your valid green card for your return; it does not replace your passport.

For Medjugorje, in Bosnia and Herzegovina, U.S. citizens do not need a visa for stays of up to 90 days. If you travel on another country's passport, check the visa requirements with the Consulate of Bosnia and Herzegovina before booking; we are happy to help you review them.

For the Holy Land, when entering through Israel, you must check whether your nationality requires a visa or an ETA-IL electronic travel authorization. Having a green card does not make you a U.S. citizen or automatically exempt you from these requirements.

At Sky Travel J&M, we review each case based on your nationality, departure city and itinerary to guide you on the documents you need to verify before traveling.
