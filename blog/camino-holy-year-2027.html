Camino de Santiago Holy Year 2027: The Holy Door and How to Gain the Jubilee

Why 2027 is special, when the Holy Door opens and how to make the pilgrimage to Santiago from the United States

Home›Blog›Camino Holy Year 2027
Pilgrimages·Pilgrimage Guides·November 21, 2026·By Doris Pérez

2027 is a Compostelan Holy Year, also called the Jacobean Holy Year or Xacobeo. It happens because July 25, 2027, the feast of Saint James the Apostle, falls on a Sunday. Throughout the year, the Holy Door of Santiago de Compostela Cathedral is open and pilgrims can gain the jubilee, that is, the plenary indulgence.

This guide explains why 2027 is special, when the Holy Door opens, how to gain the jubilee, how it differs from the Compostela and how to make the pilgrimage to Santiago from the United States. Why is 2027 a Compostelan Holy Year?

The Holy Year is celebrated whenever July 25, the feast of Saint James, falls on a Sunday. That is why it follows an irregular cycle of 6, 5, 6 and 11 years: after 2027, the next ones will be 2032, 2038 and 2049. If you want to walk in a Holy Year, 2027 is the only chance for the next five years.

What is a Holy Year on the Camino de Santiago? It is a jubilee year, a time of grace in which the Church grants a plenary indulgence to those who make a pilgrimage to the Apostle's tomb. It is called Compostelan after Santiago de Compostela and Jacobean (Xacobeo in Galician) after James, Iacobus in Latin. The previous Holy Years were 2004, 2010 and 2021, which was extended into 2022 because of the pandemic.

According to tradition, Santiago de Compostela Cathedral holds the tomb of Saint James the Greater, one of the twelve apostles. Since the Middle Ages, pilgrims from all over Europe, and today from all over the world, have walked to his tomb.

Key dates of the 2027 Holy Year

  • December 31, 2026: the Holy Door opens and the Holy Year begins.
  • July 25, 2027: the feast of Saint James, which falls on a Sunday this year.
  • September 10–20, 2027: our Camino de Santiago and Ávila pilgrimage.
  • December 31, 2027: the Holy Door closes and the Holy Year ends.

The Holy Door: when it opens and where it is

The Holy Door is on Praza da Quintana, at the back of the Cathedral, and it is opened only in Holy Years. The Archbishop of Santiago opens it in a solemn ceremony on December 31, 2026, and it stays open throughout 2027, until another ceremony closes it on December 31. Check the Cathedral's official website for the exact time of the opening.

Walking through the Holy Door is one of the most beloved gestures of the Holy Year, although it is not one of the conditions for gaining the jubilee.

How to gain the jubilee: the plenary indulgence in Santiago de Compostela

During the Compostelan Holy Year, the plenary indulgence can be gained under these conditions:

  • Visit Santiago Cathedral, where the Apostle's tomb is, and pray there: at least the Creed and the Our Father, and a prayer for the Holy Father's intentions.
  • Go to confession within fifteen days before or after the visit.
  • Receive Holy Communion.

The indulgence can be applied to yourself or to the souls in Purgatory. You don't have to walk the Camino to gain it: it is enough to make a pilgrimage to the Cathedral and fulfill the conditions. On our pilgrimage, the Pilgrim Masses along the Camino and at the Cathedral help you fulfill them without rushing.

The Compostela and the jubilee are not the same

  • The Compostela is the certificate the Pilgrim Office gives to those who walk at least the last 100 km of the Camino, with at least two stamps per day in their Pilgrim Credential on that section.
  • The jubilee is the Holy Year's plenary indulgence: a spiritual grace gained by fulfilling the conditions above. It does not require walking.

In 2027 you can receive both: walking from Sarria meets the distance for the Compostela, and on arriving at the Cathedral you can gain the jubilee.

What to expect on the Camino in 2027: more pilgrims, and when to book

The Camino is having record years. According to Pilgrim Office data, more than 530,000 pilgrims reached Santiago in 2025; the United States sent more pilgrims than any other foreign country, more than 44,000, and September was the busiest month. And 71% of U.S. pilgrims were 46 or older.

A Holy Year is expected to draw even more people, especially in summer and on the most important dates, so it pays to book several months ahead, especially if you want to walk in September, the busiest month. Traveling as a group means lodging and transportation are arranged in advance.

Walking from Sarria to Santiago: the last 100 km

Sarria is the most popular starting point because it is just over 100 km (about 70 miles) from Santiago, the minimum distance for the Compostela. The route runs through Galician villages, oak and chestnut woods and towns such as Portomarín, Palas de Rei, Melide, Arzúa and O Pedrouzo.

On our pilgrimage we walk it in six stages, at a relaxed pace and with luggage transfers between stages, so each pilgrim carries only what they need for the day. It is designed for pilgrims of all ages, including first-time walkers.

Tips for walking the Camino in September

  • Broken-in shoes: don't wear new shoes on the Camino; walk in them for a few weeks before the trip.
  • A light rain jacket: it can rain in Galicia even in September.
  • Layers: mornings are often cool and middays mild.
  • Your Pilgrim Credential: get at least two stamps a day from Sarria onward to request the Compostela.
  • Intentions: bring a written list of your family's intentions and those entrusted to you, to present to the Apostle.
