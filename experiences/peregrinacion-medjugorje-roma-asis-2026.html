Pilgrimage to Medjugorje, Rome and Assisi: a supernatural experience of faith and renewal

Our pilgrimage from September 17 to 30, 2026 to Medjugorje, Rome and Assisi was a supernatural experience that became etched in our hearts.


From the moment we arrived in Medjugorje, we felt that our Mother was waiting for us and welcoming us, surrounding us with details and wonderful people. Staying in the home of visionary Mirjana was a true gift and also a small school of spiritual growth. There we discovered that God calls us to live humility, and that if we are truly Christians, humility must have an important place in our lives.


The ascent to the Apparition Hill was unforgettable. As we prayed the rosary, we felt the presence and accompaniment of our Blessed Mother. Reaching that place was an experience full of joy and peace, and also a moment that deeply touched us.


On the way of the Way of the Cross, we felt the fatigue, difficulties and pain. But above all, each station became a journey through our own lives, accompanied by Jesus. Every word reached the depths of our hearts, healed us and encouraged us to continue forward in His hand. We walked with the hope of the Resurrection, remembering that the Way of the Cross is not the end: the goal is Easter.


The Eucharistic adorations in Medjugorje were exceptional. Seeing the large square, with capacity for thousands of people, completely silent and all united in the same spirit, was deeply moving. There we shared the best place, one that no one can take away from us: being in the presence of God. Each testimony, each confession and each shared moment will remain forever imprinted on our hearts. The confessions were renewing experiences; it is no coincidence that Medjugorje is known as the confessional of the world.


In Rome, celebrating the Eucharist in St. Peter’s Basilica, beside the tomb of Peter, was deeply meaningful to all of us. We came hand in hand with Mary, and in the chapel of the Madonna we felt that she continued to open doors and accompany us in that celebration. Every major basilica we visited touched us with its history, the faith it carries and all that it allowed us to learn and live. Knowing those places and participating in moments of prayer and adoration strengthened our faith. It was moving to think that, throughout so many centuries, the Catholic Church has remained standing.


And then there was Assisi: such a beautiful and precious place that invited us to pause and look at our lives with different eyes. This year, in which we celebrate the 800th anniversary of Saint Francis’ passing into eternal life, contemplating his example was a constant invitation to let go of what weighs us down and to live with simplicity, humility and trust in God. Francis was a man who was able to leave everything behind to follow the Lord.


The visit to St. Carlo Acutis was unforgettable and moving. Getting to know the life of a young man who had such a deep encounter with Jesus and who knew how to give the Eucharist the place it deserves touched our hearts. Being there, sharing that moment and learning from his testimony was a true gift.


We also visited St. Clare of Assisi, a woman who teaches us detachment and humility, and to place the will of God above any human desire. Her life reminded us that love for God can guide us even when it costs us to renounce what we want.


In Assisi we also visited Santa Maria degli Angeli, where the Porziuncola is located. Getting to know those places where the Lord manifested himself so specially to Saint Francis was unforgettable. Praying in the Porziuncola and visiting the cribs and the rose garden were very special moments that joined everything we lived in the Basilica of Saint Francis.


Medjugorje, Rome and Assisi left a deep mark on our history as pilgrims. We returned home with hearts full of gratitude, new bonds of fraternity and a desire to continue walking toward God. We carried with us the prayer, hope and everything we learned together, to share it with our families and communities.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which destinations did we visit during the 2026 pilgrimage?
We visited Medjugorje, Rome and Assisi. In each destination, we shared moments of prayer, encounter and fellowship that helped us renew our faith.
What experiences of faith did we share in Medjugorje?
In Medjugorje, we shared the Eucharist, moments of prayer and devotion to the Virgin Mary, the walk up Apparition Hill and testimonies that invited us to open our hearts to God.
What did Rome and Assisi mean for our pilgrimage?
Rome brought us closer to our Christian roots and the universal Church. Assisi invited us to reflect on humility, service, peace and fellowship, following the example of Saint Francis and Saint Clare.
Why was visiting Assisi in 2026 special?
2026 is the year of the special Jubilee marking the 800th anniversary of the death of Saint Francis of Assisi. This commemoration invited us to contemplate his legacy of dedication, reconciliation and care for creation.
