Trip Details

Rome

Our time in Rome begins among its squares and fountains. We will pass through Piazza Venezia and the Spanish Steps before reaching the Trevi Fountain, where anyone who wishes can follow the tradition of tossing a coin. On another day, the program brings together two sides of the city: visits to three major basilicas in the morning and the Colosseum in the afternoon.

Rome was the center of an empire that stretched across the Mediterranean. The Colosseum, completed in AD 80 under Emperor Titus, preserves the memory of the public spectacles of that era. Over the centuries, the city also became a center of Christian pilgrimage. Its basilicas and ancient monuments allow visitors to discover both histories on the same journey.

Vatican City

The itinerary includes the Papal Audience and a guided visit to St. Peter’s Basilica. Inside, Michelangelo’s Pietà offers a moment to pause and reflect. The program also includes a visit to the Vatican Museums, where the Sistine Chapel holds some of the best-known frescoes of the Renaissance.

Long before Vatican City State existed, Christians came to pray at the site venerated as the tomb of St. Peter the Apostle, above which the basilica was built. The state was established in 1929 through the Lateran Pacts, signed between the Holy See and Italy.

Venice

Discover Venice by crossing small bridges and following the canals to St. Mark’s Square. There, the Doge’s Palace recalls the days when the city ruled its maritime routes. Suggested activities include a gondola ride to see the façades from the water. The itinerary also includes a visit to Murano, known for its glass workshops.

Venice grew on the islands of a lagoon and became one of the great maritime powers of the Mediterranean. During the centuries of the Republic of Venice, known as La Serenissima, trade sustained the city and encouraged crafts such as glassmaking, a tradition that remains alive on Murano.

Florence

In Florence, suggested visits take you through the historic center from the Cathedral of Santa Maria del Fiore to Piazza della Signoria and the Ponte Vecchio. Those who would like to spend more time with art can visit the Accademia Gallery, home to Michelangelo’s David. From the Piazzale Michelangelo viewpoint, you can see the cathedral’s dome rising above the city’s rooftops.

Florence was one of the centers of the Italian Renaissance. During the 15th and 16th centuries, the support of families such as the Medici helped artists and architects leave their mark on the city. Brunelleschi’s dome and the Pitti Palace are two places that help bring that era to life.

Cinque Terre

The journey begins in Riomaggiore and Corniglia. In Riomaggiore, colorful houses cluster around a small harbor, while Corniglia offers sea views from above. The following day includes free time to enjoy the beach in Monterosso al Mare. The program then continues through the harbor of Vernazza and ends in Manarola, where the houses seem to climb the rocks.

The five villages of Cinque Terre developed along a steep stretch of the Ligurian coast. For generations, residents built stone terraces to cultivate the hillsides and adapted their homes to the terrain. This landscape, where villages and terraces reach almost to the sea, is part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Amalfi Coast

Minori will be our base for exploring the coast. On the first afternoon, the program suggests a stroll along the seafront and dinner overlooking the sea. We will then visit Amalfi, where the steps of St. Andrew’s Cathedral dominate the square, and Ravello, perched high above the coast. Before continuing to Naples, we will stop in Positano, with its colorful houses rising above the bay.

Amalfi was a maritime republic that prospered through Mediterranean trade. In Minori, the remains of a Roman villa recall an even earlier period, while Ravello preserves villas and gardens that tell the story of its past. The cultivated terraces and hillside villages form part of the landscape recognized by UNESCO.

Capri

The excursion to Capri begins with a ferry ride from Minori. Arriving by sea reveals the cliffs and the contrast between rock and water. Once on the island, its streets and terraces offer different ways to enjoy the scenery before returning to Minori.

Capri was a residence of the Roman emperor Tiberius in the 1st century AD. At the eastern end of the island stand the remains of Villa Jovis, one of his most imposing residences. Its ruins, on a promontory overlooking the Gulf of Naples, preserve traces of the baths and imperial rooms.

Naples

Naples is the final stop on the journey. We will arrive from the Amalfi Coast and spend the night here before our return flight. If arrival time allows, we can take a walk or say farewell to Italy over a Neapolitan pizza.

Naples has its roots in ancient Neapolis, founded by Greek settlers. Its history as a Mediterranean port left traces of different eras, still visible in its streets, churches and palaces. The historic center is recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
