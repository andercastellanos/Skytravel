Trip Details

Madrid

Madrid is the gateway to our pilgrimage. After the flight, meeting the group and traveling to Segovia mark the beginning of the journey. It is a good time to get to know one another and reflect on the intention each pilgrim will carry along the Camino.

Medieval Madrid dates back to the ninth century, when Emir Muhammad I established a fortress in the place known as Mayrit. Over the centuries, the settlement grew and became the seat of Philip II’s court in 1561, beginning its transformation into the capital we know today.

Segovia

In Segovia, our guided visit takes us from the Roman aqueduct to the old town, home to the Alcázar and the Cathedral. Among its squares and stone streets, the Cathedral offers a moment of quiet reflection before we continue to Galicia. Its soaring vaults and stained-glass light invite us to pause and contemplate.

The aqueduct preserves the legacy of Roman Segovia and recalls how water once reached the city. During the Middle Ages, the Alcázar became one of the residences of the kings of Castile. The present Cathedral, known as the Lady of Cathedrals, was built later. Today, the old town and aqueduct are a World Heritage Site.

Lugo

Lugo is our resting place before we begin walking the Camino. Arriving in Galicia gives us time to prepare our backpacks, share dinner with the group and get ready for the first stage. The following day, our first steps from Sarria will begin the walk.

Lugo preserves the layout of the ancient Roman city of Lucus Augusti. Its most visible legacy is the wall surrounding the historic center, now a World Heritage Site. Within the walls stands the Cathedral of Santa María, known for its tradition of permanent exposition of the Blessed Sacrament at the high altar.

Sarria

In Sarria, we take our first steps on the Camino Francés toward Portomarín. The stage leads us through Galician villages, paths lined with chestnut and oak trees, and corredoiras, the area’s traditional lanes. Between greetings of “¡Buen Camino!” and the first stretches of silence, each pilgrim begins to find their own pace.

Sarria is a Galician town linked to the Way of Saint James for centuries. Its old town preserves historic houses bearing coats of arms and the Monastery of La Magdalena, historically associated with welcoming pilgrims. Just over one hundred kilometers from Santiago de Compostela, it is now one of the best-known starting points of the Camino Francés.

Santiago de Compostela

From Monte do Gozo, we will see the Cathedral towers for the first time. After six stages on foot, we will walk through Santiago’s streets to reach Obradoiro Square together. In the Cathedral, we can venerate the tomb of Saint James the Apostle and attend the Pilgrim Mass, giving thanks for the journey and remembering the intentions we have carried along the Camino.

According to Jacobean tradition, the tomb attributed to Saint James the Apostle was discovered on Mount Libredón in the ninth century. King Alfonso II ordered the first church to be built there. Over the centuries, the arrival of pilgrims inspired the construction of the Cathedral, begun around 1075, and made Compostela one of Christianity’s great pilgrimage destinations.

Salamanca

After reaching Compostela, our guided visit to Salamanca takes us to Plaza Mayor, the Casa de las Conchas and the cathedral district. A walk through its golden-stone streets invites us to slow down and take in the surroundings. One of the most distinctive sights is the Old and New Cathedrals standing side by side, their styles telling the story of different periods in the city’s history.

In 1218, Alfonso IX of León founded the institution that became the University of Salamanca, one of the earliest in Europe. A university city grew around its classrooms, and that history remains visible in its colleges and buildings. The Old and New Cathedrals, built in different periods, also show how Salamanca’s religious architecture evolved.

Ávila

Ávila is the final stop on our pilgrimage. Its walls and stone streets frame our encounter with the story of Saint Teresa of Jesus. Learning about her life in the city where she was born invites us to pause, devote time to prayer and reflect on our journey before returning home.

Ávila is closely linked to Saint Teresa of Jesus. In 1562, she founded the Convent of San José here, the first convent of her Carmelite reform. Years later, the Convent of Santa Teresa was built on the site of her birthplace and inaugurated in 1636. Both places preserve the memory of a woman whose life of prayer and writings shaped Catholic spirituality.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the pilgrimage and how long does it last?
The pilgrimage takes place from September 10 to 20, 2027. The trip lasts 11 days and begins and ends in Madrid.
Which part of the Camino de Santiago will we walk?
We will walk six stages of the Camino Francés, from Sarria to Santiago de Compostela Cathedral, passing through Portomarín, Palas de Rei, Melide, Arzúa and O Pedrouzo. At the Cathedral, the program includes venerating the tomb of Saint James the Apostle and attending the Pilgrim Mass.
How much does the pilgrimage cost?
The land package costs USD 2,350 per person. Round-trip flights to Madrid are quoted separately, according to your departure city.
What does the pilgrimage include?

The land package includes:

  • 9 nights of accommodation with breakfast and dinner.
  • Private coach transportation according to the itinerary.
  • Luggage transfers between Camino stages.
  • Local guides for visits to Segovia, Salamanca and Ávila.
Will there be daily Mass during the pilgrimage?
Yes, there is daily Mass during the pilgrimage. We will also have time for prayer, adoration and, whenever possible, confession.
Can I travel alone and join the group?
Yes, solo pilgrims are very welcome to join the pilgrimage. Many pilgrims travel on their own. Contact us for details on joining the group.
Do you organize pilgrimages for groups?
Yes. We organize parish, family and community groups, helping with dates, flights and logistics.
